## Care needs are everyday needs

Long-term ostomy bag use changes everyday care routines. Beyond collection and emptying, wear stability, independent replacement, discretion under clothing, and concerns about leakage and odour all shape the experience.

This study starts with those daily situations to explore an ostomy bag that supports reassurance, convenience and emotional care.

![Contexts of ostomy bag wear and care](/images/projects/plant-ostomy/wearing-context.webp)

### Design priorities

- Wear stability: a supporting belt addresses concerns about loosening or exposure during movement.
- Care convenience: reduce the effort involved in positioning, connecting and removing the bag.
- Emotional experience: soft colours and a sapling motif soften the visual character of a medical device.

## From the stoma connection to the bag structure

Sketches explore component layouts and connections, focusing on the stoma interface, body fit and bottom outlet. The aim is to retain collection functionality while investigating easier attachment, removal and cleaning.

## Making the wearing sequence clearer

A rolled, adjustable opening concept explores whether unfolding the inner edge could reduce manual cutting. The connection ring, bag and supporting belt respectively provide positioning, collection and support.

![Adjustable opening concept before and after unfolding](/images/projects/plant-ostomy/adjustable-opening.svg)

![Wearing sequence for the ring, bag and supporting belt](/images/projects/plant-ostomy/wearing-sequence.webp)

### Quick attachment and sealing

A spring-and-ball mechanism positions and locks the bag onto the connection ring. Combined with a sealing ring, it explores convenient attachment and sealing at the interface. Seal performance, skin compatibility and service life still require further testing.

![Section rendering of the spring-and-ball quick connection](/images/projects/plant-ostomy/quick-release.webp)
![Connection ring and seal details](/images/projects/plant-ostomy/seal-detail.webp)

## Details for care and emotional comfort

Internal channels and barriers aim to reduce fluid flowing back towards the stoma connection during movement or while lying down. A small viewing window helps indicate when emptying is needed while limiting direct exposure of the contents. A folded bottom closure addresses daily emptying.

Plant growth provides a visual connection to emotional needs. PBAT and PLA are considered as conceptual directions for biodegradable materials. Material selection, degradation conditions and medical-waste disposal require dedicated assessment.
